# Dependence models

A vine factorizes a joint distribution into bivariate copulas. Lower trees supply the conditional coordinates used by higher trees. This gives two different ways to study temporal change. An edge can change its family or strength across time. Its conditional association can also depend on the value of another variable, and the size of this modulation can change across time.

For a continuous vine, a modulation is interpreted in the fitted variable order. A full vine can improve on a first-tree truncation even when the population log density contains only pairwise terms. Tree depth alone does not identify interaction order. For three binary spike indicators, the contrast between the two conditional pair log odds ratios can be estimated while fixing all pair margins.

Start with the [worked examples](../user-guide/vine-modulation.md) to compare these two definitions. The [evaluation guide](metrics.md) describes how to interpret the resulting quantities.
